Are transboundary water policies ready for blue-green infrastructure?

Green measures are named widely in these documents, committed to rarely, and seldom connected to the municipal level.

Two basin families supply half the European material: the Danube and Tisza with 56 passages, the Rhine, Meuse and Elbe with 41. Britain and Ireland contribute 45, a quarter of the European total, from outside the European reporting regime. The Central Asian material rests on two documents, seven of its eight passages coming from the Kazakhstan Water Code.

What the measures are

Wetland restoration, floodplain reconnection, riparian buffers and afforestation account for most of the corpus. Urban drainage appears eight times in 397 passages.

The level at which decisions are made

Documents name cities. Blue-green passages do not.

Municipal government is present throughout these documents. It is absent from the text that discusses blue-green measures.

What the connection looks like when a document makes it

Los nuevos desarrollos urbanísticos deberán minimizar la cuantía de pavimentación u ocupación impermeable a aquellas superficies en las que sea estrictamente necesario, e introducir sistemas de drenaje sostenible (uso de pavimentos permeables, tanques o dispositivos de tormenta, etc.) que garanticen que el eventual aumento de escorrentía respecto del valor correspondiente a la situación preexistente puede ser compensado o es irrelevante.
New urban developments must minimise paving and impermeable coverage to what is strictly necessary, and introduce sustainable drainage systems — permeable pavements, storm tanks and similar — ensuring that any increase in runoff against the pre-existing situation can be offset or is negligible.
Real Decreto 35/2023, Spanish river basin management plans

One passage in the corpus does all four things at once: it uses obligation language, names a specific measure, addresses an urban authority, and sets a performance test against a baseline. It is the drafting that would connect a basin plan to a city.

Questions for the research agenda

  1. The Spanish river basin plans place a binding drainage requirement on new urban development. Why is that drafting so rare, and what makes it possible there?
  2. Kazakhstan's binding commitments sit in national law rather than in its transboundary agreements. Does national legislation carry into shared-basin practice, or stop at the border?
  3. Do reporting obligations drive adoption, or documentation? Britain and Ireland left the EU regime and still supply a quarter of the European material.
